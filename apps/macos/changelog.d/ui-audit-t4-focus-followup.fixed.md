- **A click on a list gives it the keys.** A click on a Schedules row or card, a Knowledge Base tree row, or
  any part of an Action Items card now makes it current and lets ↑ ↓ move from there, even when the keys were
  elsewhere, such as the sidebar or the search field. Before, on a page just opened from the sidebar, a click
  picked the item but ↓ did nothing until Tab had once reached the list. A click into a comment editor, or
  one that selects text, keeps the keys there.
- **Tab reaches the Action Items list.** Tab from the search field now stops on the list itself, where ↑ ↓
  move through the cards. Before, Tab passed it by and landed on the first card's buttons.
- **The Schedules table keeps the keys when it stacks into cards.** At narrow widths, a click on a row opens the
  details pane and the table becomes cards. The focus now stays on the list, at the clicked schedule, instead
  of jumping to the "Table" button.
- **Switching between Schedules' Table and Cards keeps the keys on the list.** Before, the switch sent them to
  the sidebar, or with Keyboard navigation on, to the status bar.
- **One Tab stop per list.** With Keyboard navigation on, every Schedules row and card and every Knowledge Base
  tree row was a Tab stop of its own, and the Table's first row came before the list. Now Tab stops once on
  the list, before its rows, and the arrow keys (and Return in the tree) reach every item.
- **The Schedules table's focus ring is whole.** The ring on the current row was clipped to a faint line, and
  at the window's edge it disappeared. It is now drawn inside the row, like the ring on a card.
- **Action Items: the "Context note" control rings as one piece.** Its focus ring circled the small arrow
  alone, across the card's edge and through the word. Arrow and label are now one button, which a click on the
  label opens too.
- **Action Items: a ref link is one Tab stop.** A card's link to a note sat under the description as a second
  Tab stop for the same link as the chip in the card's header. While the header shows its ref chips, that
  chip is the link's one stop.
- **VoiceOver names the item the arrow keys reach.** In the Schedules list, the Action Items list and the
  Knowledge Base tree, ↑ ↓ now read out the new current item, which a list's single Tab stop otherwise left
  silent.
